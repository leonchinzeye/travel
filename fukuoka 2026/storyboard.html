福岡
Fukuoka — Cinematic Short
~90 seconds Cinematic & Moody 16 shots 4 acts
Your Kit Osmo Pocket 3 · Osmo Action 4 · Sony A7CII 24-70mm f/2.8 · iPhone 17 Pro Max — each shot below recommends the best tool for the job.
Color Grade Direction Pull highlights warm (orange/amber) · Push shadows cool (teal/green) · Slightly desaturated midtones. Think Tokyo Drift, not Instagram filter.
REF Frame Rates & Apertures Quick reference

The 180° shutter rule: always set shutter speed to double your frame rate. This gives natural motion blur — the defining look of cinematic footage.

Frame rates
24fps Cinematic. Use for most shots. Shutter → 1/50
30fps Slightly cleaner. Good fallback. Shutter → 1/60
60fps Slow-mo at 2.5× when played at 24fps. Shutter → 1/125. Use for steam, hands, crowds.
120fps Slow-mo at 5× when played at 24fps. Shutter → 1/250. Use for water, broth pours, fast action.
Sony A7CII apertures
f/2.8 Wide open. Shallow DOF, best low light. Use for steam, incense, close-ups, night.
f/4–5.6 Balanced. Good sharpness + some background blur. Use for street, yatai, shrines.
f/8–11 Everything sharp. Use for wide landscape shots, station exterior, canal reflections on tripod.
Per-camera cheat sheet
Sony A7CII Shoot 4K24 for main footage. 4K60 for slow-mo. Manual exposure always — use ND filter in daylight to hold f/2.8 + 1/50 shutter. ISO: keep under 3200, push to 6400 max.
Osmo Pocket 3 4K24 or 4K30. Use D-Log M for better grading latitude. Aperture is fixed (f/2.0) — adjust exposure via ND or ISO. ActiveTrack off for cinematic shots, on for moving subjects.
Osmo Action 4 4K30 for most shots. 4K120 for extreme slow-mo. Use Rocksteady for low-angle / moving shots. Fixed aperture f/2.8. Shoot D-Log if available for color consistency with Sony.
iPhone 17 Pro Max Use the native Camera app in Cinematic mode for rack focus shots. For slow-mo, switch to 4K120. Shoot in Apple Log (Settings → Camera → Formats) for grading flexibility. Lock exposure before recording.
Consistency tip
If you're mixing footage across cameras, shoot everything in a log profile (D-Log M on Pocket 3, D-Log on Action 4, Apple Log on iPhone, S-Log3 on Sony if available). It makes the color grade far more consistent in post.

Act 01 Arrival 0:00 – 0:20

Establish the place. No rush.

#01 Train window — city blurring past motion blur ▼
How to film

Press your phone or camera against the window to reduce vibration. Set shutter speed to 1/30s or slower (double your frame rate rule — if shooting 24fps, go to 1/50 or lower to exaggerate motion blur). Landscape orientation, horizontal. Let the city streak. Don't track anything — stay fixed.

Settings
Shutter: 1/30–1/50 Static camera Wide or normal lens Shoot through glass
Gear Osmo Pocket 3 — press it flat against the window, gimbal absorbs train vibration. iPhone works too but Pocket 3 handles the motion blur more cleanly.
Pro tip
Shoot in the direction of travel, not perpendicular — the parallel blur is more cinematic and less nauseating.
#02 Feet stepping onto platform ground-level ▼
How to film

Set your camera on the ground or as low as possible, pointing upward at a slight angle. Frame so feet enter from the left and step toward the camera. Have someone film you, or set it up and walk through yourself a few times. The gap between train and platform edge makes a natural leading line.

Settings
Low angle / ground level Wide lens Look up slightly Normal shutter
Gear Osmo Action 4 — built for ground-level, wide angle is perfect here, and it's small enough to set on the platform floor without drawing attention.
Pro tip
Shoot this twice — once with normal timing, once with slow-mo (60fps) for more control in the edit.
#03 Hakata Station exterior — golden hour hold 3–4 sec ▼
How to film

Shoot during the 30 minutes before sunset. Cross the street and get far enough back to include the full facade. Use a tripod or set it on a flat surface. Frame symmetrically — the station has a central axis. Hold the shot still for at least 8 seconds in camera so you have room to slow it down or cut leisurely.

Settings
Golden hour Tripod or stable surface Wide shot Symmetrical framing
Gear Sony A7CII 24-70mm — pull to 24mm, use a tripod or ledge. The sensor handles golden hour dynamic range far better than any of the others. This is your hero wide shot.
Pro tip
Expose for the sky, not the building — a slightly silhouetted station with a warm sky behind it is more dramatic than a flat bright exposure.
#04 Narrow alley with lanterns overhead static, let motion happen ▼
How to film

Find one of Fukuoka's covered shopping streets or lantern alleys (Kawabata or Yanagibashi area). Plant your camera at one end looking down the alley — lanterns in foreground, depth receding. Don't follow people. Let them walk through the frame naturally. Shoot for 20–30 seconds and pick the best moment in edit.

Settings
Static tripod Dusk or evening Normal or slight telephoto Let subjects pass
Gear Sony A7CII or Osmo Pocket 3 — Sony for image quality in low lamp light; Pocket 3 if you want to move through the alley smoothly without a tripod. Both work well here.
Pro tip
Shoot when it's lightly busy — not crowded, not empty. A few silhouettes walking in warm lantern light beats a busy crowd.
Act 02 The City Breathes 0:20 – 0:55

Layer urban life, food, and culture. This is the heart.

#05 Yatai stall — steam rising from ramen bowl backlit, macro or tight ▼
How to film

Position yourself so the light source (lantern, kitchen light) is directly behind the bowl. Get as close as your lens allows — fill the frame with the bowl and steam. Use a flashlight app or have a friend hold one behind to enhance the steam backlight if needed. Film at 60fps and slow down to 24fps in edit for dreamy steam effect.

Settings
60fps → slow-mo Backlit Tight / macro Manual exposure — expose for highlights
Gear Sony A7CII at f/2.8 — the 70mm end gives you compression and beautiful bokeh on the steam. Nothing else in your kit matches this for the backlit steam look.
Pro tip
Ask the chef politely before filming. Most are happy to let you get a close shot of the bowl right when it's served — the steam is most dramatic in the first 30 seconds.
#06 Chef's hands — ladle, broth pour close-up, slow motion ▼
How to film

Film at 60fps or 120fps if possible. Tight frame on just the hands and ladle — no face needed, which makes it less intrusive. Capture the moment the broth hits the bowl and the splash. Side-lit from the kitchen light is ideal. Hold camera steady or rest on the counter edge.

Settings
60–120fps Extreme close-up Side-lit Stabilize on surface
Gear iPhone 17 Pro Max — 120fps in a tight space, discreet, and the macro lens is excellent for hands. Rest it on the counter edge for stability. Sony works too if you have room.
Pro tip
Film multiple pours and picks — you'll cut to a 1–2 second clip in the edit, so volume is your friend here.
#07 Canal reflection — Nakasu district at dusk wide, symmetrical ▼
How to film

Find a bridge or bank where you can get low and include both the actual lights and their reflection in one frame. The sweet spot is 20–30 minutes after sunset — sky still has color, city lights are on. Use a tripod. Frame with the waterline roughly centered to emphasize the reflection symmetry.

Settings
Blue hour Tripod essential Wide lens Center the waterline
Gear Sony A7CII on tripod — blue hour means low light; the A7CII sensor will hold clean shadows where the other cameras will get noisy. 24mm end for the symmetrical wide.
Pro tip
A slow zoom out (2–3 seconds) from the reflection to the full scene makes a beautiful transition cut. Do a version with slow push-in too.
#08 Shrine gate (torii) — looking up silhouette works great ▼
How to film

Stand directly underneath the torii and point your camera straight up, or at a steep upward angle. Expose for the sky — let the gate go dark/silhouette. If it's overcast, this is actually ideal (even light, gate stands out). A slow tilt from the base of the gate up to the top adds movement without needing to walk.

Settings
Looking up Expose for sky Slow tilt up Silhouette okay
Gear Osmo Action 4 — the ultra-wide angle naturally exaggerates the gate's height when pointing up. Alternatively, Sony at 24mm tilted up gives a slightly more controlled, cinematic look.
Pro tip
Sumiyoshi Shrine in Hakata or Kushida Shrine are both accessible and photogenic. Early morning is best — few tourists, soft light.
#09 Incense smoke curling at a temple rack focus: smoke → face ▼
How to film

Get close to the incense burner (sensōro). Frame so smoke fills the foreground — then rack focus to someone praying or waving smoke toward themselves in the background. If your camera has manual focus, do a slow pull. If not, tap to focus on the background and let the smoke go soft in front.

Settings
Rack focus Backlit smoke Wide aperture (f/1.8–f/2.8) Side or back light
Gear Sony A7CII at f/2.8 — this shot lives and dies by depth of field. The 70mm end with a wide aperture gives you that dreamy smoke-to-face rack focus. No other camera in your kit can do this shot justice.
Pro tip
Shoot when sun is at a low angle and behind the burner — the smoke becomes luminous and almost magical against a darker background.
#10 Busy intersection — slow-mo crowd tripod, wide ▼
How to film

Find a corner or elevated position near Tenjin or Hakata station crossings. Set camera wide and static. Shoot at 60fps for slow-mo playback. Don't follow any one person — let the whole crowd flow. 30 seconds of footage gives you many usable 3–4 second clips. Alternatively, try a long exposure still and add to b-roll.

Settings
60fps slow-mo Tripod or elevated perch Wide lens Static — don't follow
Gear Osmo Pocket 3 — compact enough to hold up in a crowd without being invasive. Its 60fps slow-mo is smooth and the gimbal handles the urban bustle. iPhone also works if Pocket 3 isn't handy.
Pro tip
Tenjin underground city exits during rush hour (5–7pm) give you great crowd flow with directional light from above.
Act 03 Stillness 0:55 – 1:20

Pull back. Find the quiet.

#11 Empty early-morning street — soft light golden hour, mist if lucky ▼
How to film

Set your alarm. 6–7am in Fukuoka means streets that were packed at midnight are now near-empty. Look for long straight streets with perspective lines (power lines, shop shutters) leading to the horizon. Shoot static, wide. If there's morning mist or haze, that's a bonus — don't correct it in-camera, let it grade warm later.

Settings
Golden hour AM Static wide Leading lines Don't correct haze in-camera
Gear Sony A7CII — early morning light is soft and low-contrast; the full-frame sensor renders that haze and warmth beautifully. Use 24mm on a tripod. Osmo Pocket 3 is a decent backup if you're traveling light.
Pro tip
A lone vending machine glowing in an otherwise quiet street is a very Fukuoka/Japan image. Keep an eye out.
#12 Someone eating alone at a yatai counter respectful distance, candid ▼
How to film

Frame from outside the yatai, shooting through the open side. Use a slightly longer focal length (or crop in) so you're not in their personal space. Focus on the scene — bowl, hands, warm light — rather than the face. Don't film someone eating if they seem uncomfortable. A wide shot showing the whole yatai with a single patron is less intrusive and equally powerful.

Settings
Telephoto or crop Outside looking in Warm artificial light Candid — don't stage
Gear iPhone 17 Pro Max — the most discreet option by far. Cinematic mode gives you a natural rack focus look if you're shooting from the counter. Don't pull out the Sony here; it draws too much attention.
Pro tip
If you're eating there yourself, set your camera down on the counter pointed slightly outward — some of the best candid shots come from cameras that blend in.
#13 Ocean or park — wide, breathing room Nokonoshima or Seaside Momochi ▼
How to film

Go wide — really wide. You want sky in at least half the frame. Shoot static and just let the world breathe. Ocean with gentle waves, a lone tree in a park, the bridge at Seaside Momochi. This shot is about space and silence after the busyness of Act 2. A very slow zoom out (1cm push back over 5 seconds) works well if handheld.

Settings
Ultra wide Sky dominant Slow or static Horizon level
Gear Sony A7CII at 24mm — for the wide breathing-room shot. On the ferry, use Osmo Action 4 (waterproof, stable on a railing mount) to film the skyline receding over water.
Pro tip
Nokonoshima Island ferry ride itself is worth filming — the receding Fukuoka skyline from the water is a unique angle few visitors capture.
#14 Close-up detail — leaf, coin, menu abstract, grounding ▼
How to film

This is a wildcard — collect these throughout the trip. Look for textures, Japanese typography, worn surfaces. A 500-yen coin on a wooden counter. A menu with kanji. A moss-covered stone at a shrine. Get as close as your lens allows, focus on one small area, let everything else blur. Don't overthink it — shoot 10 options and pick the best in edit.

Settings
Macro / closest focus Wide aperture Shallow depth of field Collect throughout trip
Gear Sony A7CII at 70mm f/2.8 — closest focus distance at 70mm gives you a decent macro-like result. The shallow DOF isolates the detail beautifully. Alternatively, iPhone 17 Pro Max macro mode for truly tiny subjects.
Pro tip
Natural textures (wet stone, bamboo, sand) grade beautifully with the warm-shadows palette. Keep an eye out on rainy days especially.
Act 04 Exit 1:20 – 1:30

End on movement, not resolution.

#15 Train leaving platform — static shot symmetrical if possible ▼
How to film

Stand at the end of a platform (safely behind the line) and face down the track. Frame symmetrically with the tracks as leading lines. Let the train depart in front of you — you're watching it leave, not boarding. Hold the shot for a few seconds after the train has fully gone. That empty track is the actual closing image.

Settings
End of platform Symmetrical framing Static Hold after train departs
Gear Sony A7CII or Osmo Pocket 3 — Sony at 24mm for the widest symmetrical framing of the tracks. Pocket 3 if you want a smoother handheld hold while waiting for the train to depart.
Pro tip
Shinkansen departure at Hakata is more dramatic, but any train works. The Nishitetsu lines have a classic Japan rail look if you want something quieter.
#16 Black fade — title card (optional) hold 2 sec ▼
How to film

This is done in editing, not in-camera. Fade the last shot to black over 1.5 seconds. Hold on black for 2 seconds. If adding a title card, keep it minimal — white text on black, a Japanese character or the city name, small and centered. The music should fade with it, not cut abruptly.

Settings
Edit only Fade to black 1.5s Hold 2s Music fade with it

🎵 Music Direction

Lo-fi jazz, Japanese city pop instrumental, or something with a single piano line. Nujabes-adjacent. Look for tracks with a slow build — something that peaks around the 0:45–1:00 mark to match the Act 2 energy, then naturally winds down into stillness for Act 3–4.
