5 nights in Fukuoka — explore Hakata, Tenjin, Ohori Park, and possible day trips to Dazaifu, Itoshima, Karatsu, or Mojiko.
Day by Day
3 Jun Arrive & ease in. Check in, freshen up. Evening: head to Nakasu island's yatai strip — 20+ riverside stalls from 6pm, order tonkotsu ramen or yakitori with cold Asahi. Low-key first night. (Jun 3–6 are all walkable or by subway/Nishitetsu train — no car needed.)
4 Jun Hakata old town. Kushida Shrine (1,200-year-old spiritual heart of Fukuoka) → Sumiyoshi Shrine → Tochoji Temple (giant wooden Buddha) → Canal City Hakata for lunch. Late afternoon: walk Nakamichi Dori. Evening ramen at Shin-Shin or Hakata Ikkousha.
5 Jun Parks, coast & Tenjin. Morning walk at Ohori Park (former castle moat, Japanese Garden) → Fukuoka Castle ruins in Maizuru Park → Fukuoka Tower + Momochi Seaside Park for lunch. 4:30pm — Musashiza (Watanabedori, Chuo-ku) — irori charcoal-grill izakaya, reservation confirmed. Grilled fish, fresh seafood, drinks.
6 Jun Day trip: Dazaifu + Yanagawa. Buy the Nishitetsu Dazaifu & Yanagawa Sightseeing Pass at Tenjin Station (¥3,340/adult) — covers round-trip trains to both towns, the 70-min Yanagawa canal boat ride, and a free umegae mochi at Dazaifu. Morning: Dazaifu Tenmangu Shrine (plum gardens, Kyushu National Museum next door) — 2 hrs is plenty. Then train to Yanagawa (~1 hr). Afternoon: donkobune punt through the historic canals — iris season peaks in early June, so expect flowers lining the waterway. Finish with lunch or dinner of unagi seiro-mushi (eel steamed over rice, Yanagawa's 300-year-old local dish). Train back to Fukuoka evening.
7 Jun Day trip: Itoshima. Pick up rental car at Hakata Station first thing. Drive ~40 min west. Start at Forest Cafe Midori no Oto — treehouse-style cafe in a cedar grove in Shiraito (open 11am–3pm, Sat ✓). Next, Kobo Tottan salt workshop (Mataichi no Shio) — watch traditional multi-step sea salt production and pick up a bag to take home; located at the western tip of the Itoshima peninsula. Then Futamigaura twin rocks & torii gate at the sea → Itoshima seafood lunch (sazae-don, uni) → Shima Farm market at sunset.
8 Jun Morning free → scenic drive to Kurokawa via Aso. Check out by 9–10am. Drive south ~2 hrs to the Aso caldera. Stop at Kusasenri (草千里ヶ浜) — a sweeping volcanic grassland bowl with a pond in the middle and Nakadake smoking behind it; free to walk, horseback riding available. Then attempt Mt. Nakadake crater — access via shuttle or short walk from the carpark (check volcanic activity status on the day; closes without notice). Aso Volcano Museum is a good backup if the crater is shut. Then take the Yamanami Highway north-west (~45 min) into Kurokawa. Arrive mid-afternoon for check-in and first hot spring soak before kaiseki dinner.

Top picks: Okunoyu (stunning river bath), Yamamizuki, Okubo-ya. Morning walk through village artisan shops, small shrines, and riverside paths. Kaiseki dinner. Evening: night soak under open sky.
10 Jun Final soak → drive to Yufuin. Last morning bath and ryokan breakfast. Check out, drive via Yamanami Highway and the Aso Milk Road (volcanic caldera panoramas). ~1.5 hrs to Yufuin, arriving around noon.

Day by Day
10 Jun Arrive & explore Yufuin. Check in to Airbnb. Walk Yunotsubo Street — 50+ cafés, snack stalls (try soft cream), craft shops, and the Studio Ghibli goods store. End at Lake Kinrin for golden hour: misty reflections, koi, torii gate. Second-highest hot spring water flow in Japan.
11 Jun Day trip: Takachiho. ~1h40 drive each way — leave by 8am. Book the boat ride online in advance — opens 2 weeks ahead, sells out fast. 30-min rowboat to the base of 17m Manai Falls (¥4,100–5,100/boat, max 3 pax). After the gorge: Amanoiwato Shrine — dedicated to sun goddess Amaterasu, built over the cave where she hid; walk 10 min further to Amano Yasukawara, a sacred cave shrine where the gods are said to have gathered. Optional late evening: Night Kagura at Takachiho Shrine (8–9pm, ¥1,000/person) — four traditional sacred dances performed nightly. Catching the kagura makes it a late night back (~10:40pm), but worth it if you have the energy.
12 Jun Day trip: Beppu. ~30 min drive. Jigoku Meguri "Hells Tour" — 7 scalding geothermal pools (combo pass ¥2,000). Highlights: Umi Jigoku (vivid blue), Chinoike Jigoku (blood red), Tatsumaki geyser. At Jigokumushi Kobo Kannawa, cook your own seafood & veg using natural steam heat. Ashiyu foot baths at several hells. Allow 3.5 hrs. Arrive early to avoid tour buses.
13 Jun Final Yufuin morning → drive back. Visit Yufuin Floral Village (Cotswolds-inspired lanes, owl forest, cat café) and Comico Art Museum (Yoshitomo Nara dog sculpture). Light lunch on Yunotsubo Street. Afternoon drive ~2.5 hrs back to Fukuoka — return rental car at Hakata Station or Fukuoka Airport branch.

Food & Dining Guide
Fukuoka · Must-Eat
Hakata Tonkotsu Ramen
The original — creamy, slow-simmered pork bone broth, thin straight noodles, chashu, soft-boiled egg. Hakata is the birthplace of tonkotsu; you'll eat this multiple times.
Shin-Shin (lighter broth, locals' favourite, great yakimeshi pairing) · Hakata Ikkousha · Hakata Issou (richer, old-school) · Ichiran (customisable, private booth, good for solo)
Fukuoka · Unique to This City
Yatai Street Stalls
Fukuoka has ~100 yatai — open-air canvas stalls that appear at dusk along Nakasu and Tenjin. You sit elbow-to-elbow with strangers. Order yakitori, oden, ramen, and cold beer. Typically open 6pm–2am.
Nakasu island's southern strip (20+ stalls by the river) · Tenjin area along Watanabe Dori
Fukuoka · Local Specialties
Motsu Nabe & Mizutaki
Motsu nabe is a rich offal hot pot with cabbage, chives and garlic in a soy-miso or light broth — deeply savoury, great in groups. Mizutaki is a delicate chicken collagen hot pot, eaten with ponzu. Both are izakaya staples.
Hakata Robata Fishman (lively seafood izakaya) · Nakamuraya (old-school mizutaki) · any neighbourhood izakaya in Nakasu/Tenjin
Fukuoka · Take Home
Mentaiko
Spicy marinated pollock roe — Fukuoka's most famous product. Eat it fresh on rice, in onigiri, or as mentaiko pasta. The best gift to bring home: sealed vacuum packs from Yamaday or Fukuya shops near Hakata Station.
Fukuya (most famous brand) · Yamaday · Hakata Station food basement (depachika)
Dazaifu Day Trip
Umegae Mochi
Soft rice cakes grilled on a hot iron, stuffed with sweet red bean paste — sold by every shop on the approach to Tenmangu Shrine. Eat them fresh and hot. A ritual of the visit.
Any stall on Sando (approach street) · Kasanoya for fancier variations
Yufuin & Oita
Oita Wagyu & Onsen Tamago
Yufuin is known for Oita wagyu beef — look for wagyu croquettes and skewers on Yunotsubo Street. Onsen tamago (eggs slow-cooked in hot spring water) are a staple. Also try Yufuin soft cream — rich milk from local dairy farms.
Yunotsubo Street stalls for wagyu croquettes and soft cream · Beppu's Jigokumushi Kobo for steam-cooked seafood
Kurokawa
Kaiseki at the Ryokan
Both nights at Ryokan Sanga include multi-course kaiseki dinner — seasonal ingredients, beautifully plated, often featuring Aso beef, local river fish, mountain vegetables, and house-made tofu. Included in your booking. Breakfast is also served in-room or in the dining hall.
Included both nights at Ryokan Sanga · Kurokawa village has small cafés for lunch between bath-hops
Yanagawa Day Trip
Unagi Seiro-mushi
Yanagawa's signature dish — freshwater eel (unagi) steamed over a bed of rice in a wooden box with a sweet soy tare sauce. Invented here 300 years ago. Softer and more delicate than grilled unagi. You'll find it at restaurants all along the canal route.
Ganso Motoyoshiya (the originator) · Wakaebi · any restaurant near the Ohana garden after the boat ride
Itoshima Day Trip
Seafood by the Sea
Itoshima's coastline is one of Fukuoka's best day trips for seafood. The area is known for fresh uni (sea urchin), oysters in winter/spring, and sazae (turban shell). Several casual seafood shacks are right on the beach.
Sazae-don restaurant near Futamigaura · Shima Farm (weekend farmers' market with seafood BBQ)

Book asap — June is busy season
📄 Documents needed: International Driving Permit (IDP) — get this before departure from your home country's AA/motoring club. Bring your original home-country licence too. Passport required at pickup.
🗓 Book well in advance. June overlaps with rainy season travel and summer holiday bookings. Toyota Rent-a-Car and Nissan Rent-a-Car both have English-language booking online. Alternatively use Rakuten Travel Cars or KKDAY for English interfaces.
🛣 Navigation: Get a portable Wi-Fi or SIM (Sakura Wi-Fi / IIJmio) and use Google Maps with Japan routing. Most rental cars include a Japanese GPS — use the phone number search to find destinations easily. ETC (toll card) rental is worth it for the expressway legs.
⛽ Fuel: Japan drives on the left. Fill up at ENEOS or Cosmo stations (full-service common in rural areas — staff will pump for you and give a receipt). Most mountain routes between Fukuoka, Kurokawa, and Yufuin are well-served.
🅿 Parking in Fukuoka city: The car is most useful for day trips (Itoshima, Dazaifu by car optional). For city days, consider leaving the car at the hotel — Hakata has good subway and subway is easier than finding parking in Tenjin/Nakasu.
🏔 Mountain driving: Yamanami Highway and Aso Milk Road are scenic winding roads — beautiful but take it slow, especially in fog (common in Aso). No special licence needed, just patience. Road conditions in early June are generally fine post-rainy season.
⚠ Takachiho Gorge (Jun 11): The gorge parking fills early on weekends and holidays. Arrive by 9am or use the park-and-ride. Book the boat rental at the official Takachiho Gorge site — open for reservations 2 weeks before your date. Jun 11 is a Wednesday, so crowds should be lighter than weekends.
